### C-E01 · Day 1 · LinkedIn (text) · The onboarding checklist

Most client problems start in week one, not week ten.

A 7-step onboarding checklist that prevents most of them:

1. Proposal accepted in writing
2. Agreement signed (scope, exclusions, payment terms, revisions)
3. Deposit invoice sent. Work starts when it clears
4. Welcome email with numbered next steps
5. Intake form instead of 20 questions by email
6. Communication norms agreed: where, when, how fast
7. Kickoff call with an agenda, plus a written summary the same day

Same steps, same order, every client. After three clients you'll know which questions to add to your form.

### C-E03 · Day 5 · LinkedIn (text) · Payment reminders, decided once

Chasing invoices feels personal because most of us decide what to do each time.

Decide once:

A: 3 days before due: friendly heads-up (catches wrong-inbox problems)
B: due date: short reminder
C: +7 days: "anything holding it up?"
D: +14 days: ask for a payment date in writing
E: +30 days: final notice, stating only what's in your agreement

It matters: in Bonsai's data from 100,000+ freelancers, 29% of invoices were paid at least a day late (Bonsai, updated Jan 2026).

### C-E05 · Day 9 · LinkedIn (text) · Why AI drafts sound generic

If your AI drafts sound generic, the problem usually isn't the prompt. It's missing context.

The assistant doesn't know your services, your terms, or the promises you never make. So it fills the gaps with the average of everything it's read.

Fix it once with a one-page business brief:
→ Who I am
→ Services
→ Ideal clients
→ How I work
→ Policies
→ Tone and words to avoid

Let the AI interview you to build it (about 20 minutes), then reuse it everywhere.

### C-E07 · Day 13 · LinkedIn (text) · Scope creep in one email

Scope creep rarely arrives as a big request. It's "while you're in there…"

The goal isn't to say no. It's to make the change visible:

"Thanks for the idea. It's outside the current scope, so two options:
1) Add it now: [price], and launch moves to [date]
2) Keep the launch date and do it as a follow-up phase
I'll hold off on this piece until I hear which you prefer."

That last line matters most. Starting "just to be helpful" makes it very hard to charge for later.

### C-E11 · Day 21 · LinkedIn (text) · Deposits and milestones

Payment problems are usually decided before the project starts.

Three decisions to make once and put in every proposal:

1. Deposit: "Work starts once the deposit has cleared." (Then stick to it.)
2. Milestones clients can see: "design approved", not "50% complete"
3. Terms with a real date on every invoice: "Due 14 October", not just "Net 14"

Why: in Bonsai's data, invoices over $20,000 were 3× more likely to be late than invoices under $100. Smaller, earlier payments mean less at risk at any moment.
