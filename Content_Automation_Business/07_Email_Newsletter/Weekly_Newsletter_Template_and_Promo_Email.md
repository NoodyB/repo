## Weekly newsletter template ("One idea a week")

**Send:** same day and time every week (suggested Tuesday morning in your main audience's time zone). Keep it to 300–450 words.

```text
Subject: [Specific outcome in plain words, ≤ 50 characters]
   (e.g., "A 7-field inquiry form", not "Newsletter #12")
Preview text: [One sentence that finishes the subject's thought]

Hi [FIRST NAME],

[THE PROBLEM, 2–3 sentences]
A specific admin moment readers recognize. No stats unless sourced.

[THE IDEA, 3–6 short paragraphs or a list]
One system, template, checklist or prompt. Include the thing itself,
not just a link to it. Readers should be able to use it from the email alone.

[TRY THIS WEEK, 1–2 sentences]
One small action with a time estimate ("10 minutes: …").

[GO DEEPER, 1 link]
→ [Article title]([SITE]/blog/…)

[OPTIONAL, 1 line, at most every 3rd email]
A mention of a product or the bundle, clearly as our own product.

[SIGN-OFF]
[YOUR NAME]

P.S. [Reply prompt: "What's your version of this? Hit reply."]
---
Footer: why they're receiving it · postal address · unsubscribe
```

**Rules**
- One idea per email. If you have two, that's next week's email.
- Sourced facts only, with the date. No invented results, testimonials or income claims.
- Product mentions at most one email in three, labeled as our product.
- Affiliate links only if issued and disclosed (see `06_Affiliate_Marketing`).

**Filled example**

> **Subject:** A 7-field inquiry form
> **Preview:** Every extra required field is a reason not to submit.
>
> Hi Sam,
>
> Long inquiry forms feel thorough. For the person filling them in, they feel like homework, and some people just close the tab.
>
> Seven fields are enough to qualify a lead: name; email; business and website; what they need help with; timeline; budget range (including "not sure yet"); anything else. Then end with when they'll hear from you: "I reply within one business day."
>
> **Try this week (10 minutes):** open your inquiry form and delete or make optional anything that isn't on that list.
>
> → [Tally vs Google Forms vs Jotform for intake forms]([SITE]/blog/client-intake-form-tools/)
>
> [YOUR NAME]
>
> P.S. What's the one question you always ask new leads? Hit reply.
