## Email 1: Day 2 · the privacy habit

**Subject:** Before you paste that email thread into ChatGPT…
**Preview text:** The five-second habit that matters more than any setting.

Hi [FIRST NAME],

The fastest way to get value from the starter kit is to paste real client situations into an AI assistant. The fastest way to get into trouble is to paste them *as they are*.

**The five-second habit:** before pasting, replace names, emails, amounts and anything confidential with placeholders: [CLIENT NAME], [AMOUNT], [DATE]. The AI writes just as good an email to "[CLIENT NAME]". You swap the real details back in before sending.

**Never paste:** passwords or access links, bank or card details, ID numbers, health/legal/HR details about real people, or anything a client marked confidential.

Then check your assistant's data settings. I wrote up where they are in ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini (checked against their help pages): **[Read the privacy guide]([SITE]/blog/client-data-privacy-ai/)**

## Email 2: Day 4 · make it sound like you

**Subject:** Why AI emails sound like AI emails
**Preview text:** "I hope this finds you well" and how to never see it again.

Hi [FIRST NAME],

You can usually spot an AI-written email in the first line. So can your clients.

The fix is a **voice profile**: a short description of how *you* write, built from 3–5 of your real emails (names removed). You ask the AI to describe your sentence length, greetings, sign-offs, formality, the things you never do, and how you deliver bad news, and to **not invent traits that aren't in the samples.**

Save it next to your business brief (SET-01 in your kit), and add both to every drafting prompt.

**[The voice profile prompt and an example]([SITE]/blog/make-ai-sound-like-you/)**

## Email 3: Day 7 · the weekly review

**Subject:** One appointment a week for all your admin
**Preview text:** 45 minutes, five blocks, and admin stops leaking into every day.

Hi [FIRST NAME],

Admin with no set time leaks into every day. The fix is one appointment a week:

1. **Clear the decks** (10 min): inbox processed, tasks captured
2. **Pipeline** (10 min): every client has a next action and a date
3. **Money** (10 min): send invoices, send due reminders, record payments
4. **Next week** (10 min): block time, prepare client updates
5. **Improve one thing** (5 min): turn one annoying task into a template

Your starter kit has a printable 30-minute version (page 8), and prompt OPS-05 can pre-sort your week for you.

**Your homework:** put a recurring 45-minute block in your calendar this week. Friday afternoon and Monday morning both work well.

## Email 4: Day 10 · getting paid

**Subject:** Decide your payment reminders once
**Preview text:** Five stages, so chasing invoices stops feeling personal.

Hi [FIRST NAME],

Chasing invoices feels awkward because we decide what to do each time. Decide once instead:

- **A:** 3 days before due: friendly heads-up
- **B:** due date: short reminder
- **C:** +7 days: "anything holding it up?"
- **D:** +14 days: ask for a payment date in writing
- **E:** +30 days: final notice (only consequences that are in your agreement)

In Bonsai's data from over 100,000 freelancers, 29% of invoices were paid at least a day late (updated January 2026), so it's worth a system.
