# Brand decision: one brand for both businesses

**Recommendation: use the same brand, Band of One, for the content business and the digital products business.**

| Consideration | Shared brand | Separate content brand |
|---|---|---|
| Customer recognition | A reader who trusts an article recognizes the same name on the product page | Readers must be introduced to a second brand at the moment of purchase |
| Workload | One logo, one palette, one set of social accounts, one newsletter | Two of everything |
| Cost | One domain | Two domains |
| SEO & authority | Links and mentions build one site's authority | Authority is split |
| Risk | If the brand name must change, both businesses change together (mitigated: the name lives in one config file) | Lower coupling |
| Flexibility | Can still create sub-series (e.g., "Band of One Field Notes" for the newsletter) | Full freedom |

The shared brand wins on every factor that matters at this stage: recognition, workload and cost.

# Target audience profile

These are **illustrative composites built from the research**, not real customers. Use them to decide what to write; never present them as testimonials.

## Primary: The overbooked specialist

- **Who:** A freelance designer, developer, writer or marketer with 2–8 active clients. Has been independent for 1–5 years.
- **Situation:** Plenty of work, but admin happens at night: follow-ups, invoices, onboarding emails, scope conversations.
- **Goals:** Protect billable time; look professional; get paid on time; stop reinventing the same emails.
- **Frustrations:** Generic AI output; tool overload; late payments; guilt about "not having systems".
- **What they need from us:** Copy-paste workflows, templates, and a simple tracker. Short videos on the go, then a deeper article.
- **Where they are:** YouTube, Instagram, LinkedIn, Reddit freelance communities, newsletters.

## Secondary: The new consultant or coach

- **Who:** Recently left a job to consult or coach. Strong domain expertise, little operations experience.
- **Goals:** Set up a credible client process from day one.
- **What they need:** An onboarding system, proposal structure, first SOPs, and guidance on which tools are worth paying for.

## Tertiary: The service provider curious about AI

- **Who:** A bookkeeper, VA, photographer or similar. Uses email and spreadsheets; hasn't used AI much for work. This matches the Census finding that under 20% of the smallest firms reported using AI.
- **What they need:** Plain-language first steps, privacy guidance, and a small number of prompts that work.

**Out of scope:** Agencies with staff, enterprise teams, and "make money online" seekers. We don't write for them.

# Unique value proposition

**Practical AI systems for businesses of one.**
*Workflows, templates and honest tool notes for people who run a service business on their own.*

Supporting proof points (things we can truthfully show):
1. Every article contains a usable artifact: template, checklist or prompt.
2. Reviews state whether a tool was hands-on tested or researched.
3. Recommendations are tool-agnostic, and free options are always mentioned.

# Editorial style guide

## Article anatomy (every post)

1. **Title:** the job to be done, 45–65 characters. Example: *"How to Onboard a New Client in 30 Minutes (With AI Doing the Typing)"*.
2. **Intro (≤120 words):** the situation, the cost of the problem, and what the reader will have by the end.
3. **Quick answer box:** the 3–5 step summary for skimmers.
4. **Body:** H2 sections that follow the workflow order. Each step has an example.
5. **Artifact:** a template, prompt or checklist in a highlighted block.
6. **Pitfalls / what not to automate.**
7. **Sources:** for any statistic or factual claim.
8. **CTA:** one primary CTA (the lead magnet or a relevant product) plus 2–4 internal links.
9. **Metadata:** meta description (140–160 characters), slug, category, tags, "last updated" date.

## Style rules

- US English. Sentence-case headings. Oxford comma.
- Numerals for 10 and above; percentages as "29%".
- Dates: "24 September 2026" in prose; ISO `2026-09-24` in metadata.
- Tools: official capitalization (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Google Sheets, Make, n8n).
- Prompts appear in monospace blocks with `[PLACEHOLDERS]` in square brackets and caps.
- Reading level: aim for grade 7–9 (plain language) without dumbing down.
- Length: as long as the job needs. Typical guides run 1,200–2,200 words; don't pad.

## Honesty and compliance rules

- **Tested vs researched:** reviews carry a label, either "Hands-on tested on [date]" or "Researched from public documentation on [date]; not hands-on tested."
- **Affiliate disclosure:** placed near the top of any post with affiliate links, and repeated next to the first link (see `06_Affiliate_Marketing/Affiliate_Disclosure_Language.md`).
- **No invented experiences:** we never write "I used this for 6 months" unless you did and it's documented.
- **Legal/financial topics:** educational framing, no jurisdiction-specific legal claims, and a "consider a professional" note.
- **AI assistance disclosure:** the About page states that AI helps draft content and a human reviews every piece before publishing.

# Visual guidelines for content

- **Article hero images:** generated from our template (Paper background, Teal disc, Orange band, Fraunces title). No stock photos of people.
- **Short-form video:** 1080×1920. Title card in the top third (safe area), captions burned in using Inter Bold on an Ink pill with 85% opacity, brand mark in the corner at 6% width.
- **Carousels / educational posts:** 1080×1350. Slide 1 = hook in Fraunces 72px; slides 2+ = one idea each; final slide = save/share + link-in-bio CTA.
- **Pinterest pins:** 1000×1500. Headline top, template preview middle, domain bottom.
- **Thumbnails (YouTube):** 1280×720. Maximum 5 words, high contrast (Ink/Paper), one Orange accent.

Templates for all of these are generated by `Build_Tools` into `05_Social_Media_Content/templates/`.
