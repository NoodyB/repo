An intake form does two jobs: it **pre-qualifies inquiries** before you spend time on a call, and it **collects onboarding answers** in a consistent format so you're not digging through email threads. Both are high-value, low-effort improvements for a solo business.

Three tools come up most for this: **Tally**, **Google Forms** and **Jotform**. All have free options. Here's how they compared on paper, and what to put in the form itself.

**How we researched this:** official pricing pages, checked 24 September 2026. This is a **researched** comparison, not a hands-on test. We have **no affiliate relationship** with any of these tools at the time of writing.

## The short version

| | **Tally** | **Google Forms** | **Jotform** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free plan (checked 24 Sept 2026) | Unlimited forms and submissions | Free with a Google account | Starter: 5 forms, 100 monthly submissions, 500 total submission storage |
| Feel | Document-style editor, modern look | Simple, familiar | Feature-rich, many templates |
| Best for | Polished forms without paying | Quick internal or simple forms | Complex forms, if you stay within limits or pay |

## Tally

**Free plan (checked 24 Sept 2026):** Tally's pricing page described its free plan as "free forever" with unlimited forms and submissions, plus features including conditional logic, file uploads, signatures and payments. The Pro plan was listed at $24 a month (monthly billing), with two months off for yearly billing.

**Good fit if:** you want professional-looking intake and onboarding forms without a subscription. The generous free plan suits a solo business well.

**Worth knowing:** Tally also runs a referral program (20% of subscription payments, capped at $150 per referred user, according to its help center). We mention it for transparency; we're not in it.

## Google Forms

**Free:** included with a Google account.

**Good fit if:** you already live in Google Workspace and want answers flowing straight into Google Sheets with minimal setup. It's simple and reliable.

**Watch out for:** it looks plainer than the alternatives, and branding options are limited. That's fine for onboarding questionnaires, but less polished for a public inquiry form.

## Jotform

**Free Starter plan (checked 24 Sept 2026):** 5 forms, 100 monthly submissions and 500 total submission storage.

**Good fit if:** you need a large template library and advanced form features, and your volume fits the free limits (or you're prepared to pay).

**Watch out for:** 100 monthly submissions and 500 total storage can be reached over time. Check the current limits against your expected volume.

## Which should you choose?

- **Want polished forms for free, with room to grow:** Tally
- **Want the simplest link to Google Sheets:** Google Forms
- **Need advanced features and have low volume:** Jotform

## What to ask on an inquiry form

Keep it short: **7 fields or fewer** is a good target, because every extra required field is a reason not to submit.

1. Name
2. Email
3. Business name and website
4. What do you need help with? (short text or multiple choice of your services)
5. Ideal timeline
6. Budget range (ranges, including "Not sure yet")
7. Anything else I should know?

End with a confirmation message that says **when they'll hear from you**: "Thanks! I reply to every inquiry within one business day."

## What to ask on an onboarding questionnaire

Sent after the client says yes (see the [client onboarding checklist](/blog/client-onboarding-checklist/)):

- Main goals for the project, in their words
- Audience or customers
- Examples they like and dislike (links)
- Brand assets and where to find them
- Who approves work, and who pays invoices (plus any PO requirements)
- Key dates or constraints
- Preferred communication channel

**Don't ask for passwords in a form.** Ask clients to share access through a password manager or the platform's own user-invite feature.

## Connect it to your system

- Send answers to a **spreadsheet** or tracker, so each inquiry becomes a row with a next action (see [spreadsheet vs Notion vs CRM](/blog/spreadsheet-vs-notion-vs-crm/)).
- If you automate anything, start with "form response → tracker row + notification to me" (see [what to automate first](/blog/what-to-automate-first/)).
- Reply with a saved template personalized by AI. The [12 AI prompts for client emails](/blog/ai-prompts-for-client-emails/) include one for new inquiries.

**The takeaway:** any of the three will do the job. Pick the one whose free plan fits your volume, keep the form short, and connect the answers to one tracker.
