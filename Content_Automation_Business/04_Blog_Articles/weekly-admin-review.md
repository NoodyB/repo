When admin has no set time, it leaks into every day: a quick invoice here, a follow-up there, a nagging feeling that something's been forgotten. A **weekly admin review** gives admin one appointment. You deal with it properly once a week, and it stops interrupting the rest.

Here's a 45-minute agenda, what to check in each block, and how to make it a habit.

## Why weekly

Weekly is frequent enough that nothing gets badly overdue (a follow-up, an invoice reminder, a lead going cold) and rare enough to be worth doing properly. It also matches the rhythm of [weekly client updates](/blog/weekly-client-update-template/), which fit naturally into the same session.

Admin is a real share of the week for many business owners. In Time etc's 2023 survey of 251 US entrepreneurs, respondents reported an average of 36% of their work week going on admin tasks. Batching it makes that time predictable.

## The 45-minute agenda

| Block | Minutes | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Clear the decks** | 10 | Process inbox and messages; capture tasks into your tracker |
| **2. Pipeline** | 10 | Check every client's stage and next-action date; send due follow-ups |
| **3. Money** | 10 | Invoices to send; reminders due; payments to record |
| **4. Next week** | 10 | Block time for client work and deadlines; prepare update notes |
| **5. Improve one thing** | 5 | Turn one annoying task into a template, SOP or prompt |

### 1. Clear the decks (10 min)

- Get your inbox to a state where nothing important is buried. Archive, reply, or turn emails into tasks.
- Capture every open loop ("send Maya the logo files") into one list or tracker.
- Note anything you're waiting on from a client, with a follow-up date.

### 2. Pipeline (10 min)

- Every lead and client should have a **next action and date**. Anything without one gets one.
- Send follow-ups that are due: quiet inquiries, proposals awaiting a decision.
- Glance at the whole pipeline. Is next month's work lined up, or is it time to do some marketing?

### 3. Money (10 min)

- **Invoice** any milestone approved this week.
- **Check overdue invoices** and send whichever reminder is due. A [five-stage reminder schedule](/blog/payment-reminder-schedule/) takes the guesswork out, and a [spreadsheet tracker](/blog/track-invoices-in-a-spreadsheet/) can tell you exactly which reminder each invoice needs.
- **Record payments** received.

This block matters because late payment is common. Bonsai's data from over 100,000 freelancers found 29% of invoices were paid at least a day late (Bonsai, updated January 2026). A weekly check means nothing drifts for a month before you notice.

### 4. Next week (10 min)

- Block time in your calendar for client deliverables and deadlines, focus work first.
- Draft (or prepare notes for) client updates due on your update day.
- Check for clashes: two deadlines on the same day, a client waiting on something from another.

### 5. Improve one thing (5 min)

Pick one repeated annoyance from this week and make it easier next time:

- Save an email you rewrote as a **template**
- Write a quick [SOP](/blog/sop-template-one-person-business/) for a process you had to think through
- Save a prompt that worked well

Small, compounding improvements. This block is what turns a review into a system.

## Let AI do the sorting (optional)

If you keep your tracker, invoice list and calendar in text form, an AI assistant can pre-sort them. Redact client names and amounts first:

```text
Act as my weekly admin review assistant. I'll paste: my client tracker rows
(redacted), my open invoices with due dates, and my calendar for next week. Produce:
(1) follow-ups due this week, with the template to use; (2) invoice actions by
reminder stage; (3) risks (overdue items, client dependencies, deadline clashes);
(4) a suggested time-block plan for next week; (5) one improvement to my admin
system based on patterns you notice. Ask me questions if something is unclear.
Data: """[PASTE]"""
```

Treat the output as a checklist to verify, not instructions to follow blindly.

## Track four numbers

Once a month, note:

1. **Hours on admin this week** (a rough estimate is fine)
2. **Overdue invoices** (count and total)
3. **Average time to first reply** on new inquiries
4. **Proposals sent vs. accepted**

Watching these over two or three months shows whether your system is working, far better than any promise could.

## Make it stick

- **Same time, every week.** Many people like Friday afternoon (close the week) or Monday morning (set it up).
- **Put it in your calendar** as a recurring appointment, and treat it like a client meeting.
- **Use the same checklist** each time. Print the agenda above or save it as a template.
- **Keep it to 45 minutes.** If it regularly runs over, that's a sign something needs a template or an SOP.

**The takeaway:** one 45-minute appointment a week keeps your pipeline moving, your invoices paid and your admin out of the rest of your week.
