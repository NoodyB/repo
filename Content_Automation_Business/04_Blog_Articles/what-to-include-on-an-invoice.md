An invoice has one job: get you paid, on time, without questions. Many late payments start with an invoice that's missing something: no due date, a vague description, the wrong recipient, no easy way to pay. Each gap gives the client's accounts process a reason to set it aside.

Here's a checklist of what to include, why each item matters, and where to check the legal requirements for your country.

## The checklist

### Identification

- [ ] **The word "Invoice"** and a **unique invoice number** (sequential, never reused, e.g., 2026-014)
- [ ] **Invoice date**
- [ ] **Your name and business name**, address and contact details
- [ ] **The client's business name and address**, and the person or department it's for

### What you're charging for

- [ ] **A clear description** of the work: "Website design: milestone 2 (design approved 3 Oct)" rather than "Services"
- [ ] **Date(s) the work was done** or delivered
- [ ] **Amounts**: line items, quantities or hours if relevant, subtotal
- [ ] **Tax** (VAT, GST or sales tax) if you're registered and it applies, shown separately
- [ ] **Total amount due**, with currency

### Getting paid

- [ ] **The due date as an actual date** ("Due 14 October 2026"), not just "Net 14"
- [ ] **How to pay**: bank details, a payment link, or both
- [ ] **Reference to use** with the payment (usually the invoice number)
- [ ] **PO number** if the client requires one
- [ ] **Your payment terms**, as agreed, and any late-payment terms *only if they're in your agreement and allowed where you work*

## What the law requires (it varies)

Invoice requirements depend on your country, whether you're registered for VAT, GST or sales tax, and sometimes on your business structure. Your tax authority's website is the place to check.

As one example, the **UK government** lists what an invoice must include: a unique identification number; your company name, address and contact information; the name and address of the customer; a clear description of what you're charging for; the date the goods or service were provided; the invoice date; the amounts charged; VAT if applicable; and the total owed. Sole traders must also include their name and any business name, plus an address where legal documents can be delivered if they use a business name (GOV.UK, checked 24 September 2026). VAT-registered businesses have further requirements for VAT invoices.

Even if you're not in the UK, that list is a sensible baseline, because it's also what a client's accounts team needs to process your invoice without asking questions.

## Why each detail helps you get paid

| Detail | What it prevents |
|---|---|
| Unique number | "Which invoice is this?" and duplicate payments |
| Clear description | "What's this for?" and approval delays |
| Right recipient | Invoices sitting in the wrong inbox |
| Due date as a date | Ambiguity about when "Net 14" started |
| Payment link or bank details | Friction at the moment of payment |
| PO number | Automatic rejection by some accounts departments |

Late payment is common. In Bonsai's data from over 100,000 freelancers, 29% of invoices were paid at least a day late (Bonsai, updated January 2026). You can't control a client's cash flow, but you can remove every reason your invoice might be set aside.

## Invoice numbering that stays tidy

Use a format that sorts correctly and never repeats, such as `YYYY-NNN` (2026-001, 2026-002, …). Don't restart per client, and don't reuse a number from a cancelled invoice. Issue a credit note instead, if your accounting tool supports it.

## Send it well

- **Send it promptly**: on the day a milestone is approved, not at the end of the month.
- **Use a clear subject line**: "Invoice 2026-014 – [Project] – due 14 Oct".
- **Attach a PDF** even if your tool sends a link. Some accounts teams require attachments.
- **Log it** in your tracker with the due date, so your [reminder schedule](/blog/payment-reminder-schedule/) runs on time.

## Track what's outstanding

Once invoices are out, you need to see at a glance what's due, what's overdue and which reminder to send. A simple spreadsheet does the job. See our [tested spreadsheet formulas for tracking invoices and reminders](/blog/track-invoices-in-a-spreadsheet/), which include a free starter sheet.

## A quick template layout

```text
INVOICE 2026-014                          Invoice date: 30 September 2026
                                          Due date:     14 October 2026
From: [Your name / business name]
      [Address] · [Email]

To:   [Client business name]
      [Address] · Attn: [Accounts contact] · PO: [if required]

Description                                   Qty    Rate      Amount
Website design – milestone 2 (approved 29 Sep)  1   2,000.00  2,000.00
                                              Subtotal         2,000.00
                                              [Tax, if applicable]
                                              TOTAL DUE        2,000.00 [CUR]

Pay by bank transfer: [details] · or online: [payment link]
Please use reference 2026-014.
Payment terms: 14 days, as agreed in our contract of [date].
```

**The takeaway:** a complete invoice is the cheapest late-payment prevention there is. Use the checklist once to build your template, then every invoice is right by default.
