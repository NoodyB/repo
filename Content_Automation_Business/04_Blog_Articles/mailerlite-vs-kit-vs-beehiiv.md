For a service business, an email list isn't about building a media empire. It's about staying in touch with past clients, warm leads and people who might need you in six months. A short, useful email every week or two keeps you top of mind without constant social posting.

Three platforms come up again and again for small creators and businesses: **MailerLite**, **Kit** (formerly ConvertKit) and **beehiiv**. They're built for different things. Here's how their free plans compared when we checked.

**How we researched this:** official pricing pages, checked 24 September 2026. This is a **researched** comparison, not a hands-on test. We have **no affiliate relationship** with these providers at the time of writing.

## The short version

| Free plan (checked 24 Sept 2026) | **MailerLite** | **Kit** | **beehiiv** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Subscribers | 250 | Up to 10,000 | 2,500 |
| Sending | 2,500 emails/month | Unlimited broadcasts | Unlimited sends |
| Automations / welcome sequence | 3 automations | Visual automation builder and automated sequences **not** on free | Automations/welcome sequences **not** on free (Launch plan) |
| Landing pages / forms | 1 landing page, 3 signup forms | Unlimited landing pages and forms | Website included |
| Built for | Small businesses | Creators | Newsletter publishers |
| Cheapest paid (as listed) | Comfort $10.80/month billed yearly (500 subscribers) | Creator $33/month billed yearly (1,000 subscribers) | $43/month |

Plans and limits change often. Check each pricing page before you decide.

## What a service business actually needs

Before comparing features, decide what you need in the first six months:

1. **A signup form** on your website
2. **A welcome email** that delivers your free resource (a checklist, template or guide)
3. **A short onboarding sequence**: a few emails over the first two weeks introducing who you are and how you help
4. **Regular broadcasts**: your weekly or fortnightly email

Item 3 is the deciding factor. It needs **automation**, and that's where the free plans differ most.

## MailerLite: automation on the free plan, small list limit

**Free plan (checked 24 Sept 2026):** 250 subscribers, 2,500 emails a month, 3 automations, 1 landing page, 3 signup forms and 1 website. The Comfort plan was listed at $10.80 a month billed yearly (or $12 monthly) for 500 subscribers.

**Good fit if:** you want a **welcome sequence from day one** without paying. It's the only one of the three whose free plan listed automations.

**Watch out for:** the 250-subscriber limit. That's plenty to start, but plan for the paid tier as your list grows.

## Kit: generous list size, automations are paid

**Free plan (checked 24 Sept 2026):** up to 10,000 subscribers, unlimited landing pages and forms, unlimited broadcasts, tagging and segmentation, and the ability to sell digital products (with a transaction fee). The visual automation builder and automated email sequences were **not** included on the free plan. The Creator plan was listed at $33 a month billed yearly for 1,000 subscribers.

**Good fit if:** you'll mainly send **broadcasts** and want room to grow a larger list for free, and you're happy to add automations later on a paid plan.

## beehiiv: built for publishing

**Free "Launch" plan (checked 24 Sept 2026):** 2,500 subscribers, unlimited sends, a website and custom domain support. Automations and welcome sequences were not included. The cheapest paid plan was listed at $43 a month.

**Good fit if:** the newsletter itself is a core product: you want a publication-style website and growth features built for newsletter publishers.

**Watch out for:** for a service business that mainly needs a welcome sequence and occasional broadcasts, it may be more platform than you need.

## Also worth knowing: Brevo

Brevo's free plan listed 500 contacts but did not include marketing automation or landing pages (checked 24 Sept 2026). It's worth a look if you also need transactional email, but for a simple welcome sequence, MailerLite's free plan is more directly suited.

## Our recommendation for most service businesses

- **Starting from zero, want a welcome sequence:** **MailerLite** free, then upgrade as the list grows.
- **Broadcast-only, expect a bigger list:** **Kit** free.
- **Newsletter is the product:** **beehiiv**.

Whichever you choose:

- **Get consent properly.** Only add people who signed up. Never import clients or contacts without permission, and make unsubscribing easy. Rules differ by country, so check the requirements where you and your subscribers are.
- **Deliver what you promised** in the welcome email, immediately.
- **Keep it useful.** One practical idea per email beats a digest of links.

## A simple first sequence

1. **Welcome** (immediately): deliver the free resource, and say what to expect and how often
2. **Day 2:** your best practical tip
3. **Day 5:** a short story of a common problem and how to fix it
4. **Day 9:** a useful template or checklist
5. **Day 14:** how you (or your products) can help, low-pressure

Keep each email short, and write it in your own voice. An [AI voice profile](/blog/make-ai-sound-like-you/) helps with drafts.

**The takeaway:** MailerLite if you need a free welcome sequence now, Kit if you want a large free list for broadcasts, beehiiv if the newsletter is the business. Check current limits before committing.
