The usual advice is "automate your admin". The usual result is a weekend spent building a clever automation for a task you do twice a month, while the tasks that actually eat your week carry on untouched.

Automation is only one of five things you can do with an admin task, and often not the best one. This guide gives you a simple scoring method to find the tasks worth fixing first, and to pick the right fix for each.

## Start with an honest list

List every recurring admin task you did in the last month. Look through your sent email, calendar and task list to jog your memory. Typical entries:

- Replying to inquiries
- Writing proposals
- Onboarding new clients
- Weekly client updates
- Meeting notes and follow-ups
- Sending invoices
- Chasing late payments
- Social media posts
- Bookkeeping and receipts

It adds up. In a 2023 survey of 251 US entrepreneurs by Time etc, respondents reported spending an average of 36% of their work week on admin tasks. Your list shows where *your* share goes.

## Score each task

For each task, note four things:

1. **Times per month**
2. **Minutes each time**
3. **Repeatability (1–3):** 3 = almost identical every time; 2 = similar with some variation; 1 = different every time
4. **Risk (1–3):** 1 = low stakes, easy to fix; 2 = medium (money, client data, deadlines); 3 = high (legal, sensitive or relationship-critical)

Then calculate:

> **Priority score = (times per month × minutes each) × repeatability ÷ risk**

High scores are **frequent, time-consuming, repeatable and low-risk** tasks. Fix those first.

## A worked example (fictional)

Sam, a freelance web designer with four active clients:

| Task | Times/mo | Min each | Repeat | Risk | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Weekly client updates | 16 | 20 | 3 | 1 | **960** |
| Social media posts | 8 | 30 | 2 | 1 | 480 |
| Replying to inquiries | 8 | 15 | 3 | 1 | 360 |
| Onboarding emails & setup | 2 | 60 | 3 | 1 | 360 |
| Writing proposals | 3 | 90 | 2 | 2 | 270 |
| Meeting notes & follow-ups | 12 | 15 | 3 | 2 | 270 |
| Sending invoices | 6 | 15 | 3 | 2 | 135 |
| Chasing late payments | 3 | 20 | 3 | 2 | 90 |

The surprise: the "small" weekly updates add up to more time than proposals, and they score highest because they're identical in structure and low-risk. That's where Sam starts. Not with a complex automation, but with a [four-part update template](/blog/weekly-client-update-template/) and an AI prompt.

## Choose the right fix

Each task gets one of five decisions:

| Decision | Use when | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Eliminate** | Nobody would notice if you stopped | A monthly report no client reads |
| **Template** | The task is nearly identical every time | Invoice cover emails |
| **AI draft** | The structure repeats but the content changes | Proposals, updates, call summaries |
| **Automate** | It's rule-based, frequent, low-risk and already templated | Logging new form submissions to your tracker |
| **Keep human** | High risk, high judgment or relationship-critical | Pricing conversations, complaints, bad news |

Notice the order. **Automation comes after templating.** If you can't write the process down as a checklist (an [SOP](/blog/sop-template-one-person-business/)), you're not ready to automate it. Automating a messy process just produces messy results faster.

## Good first automations for solo businesses

When a task does earn automation, start small and low-risk:

- **Intake form → tracker row:** new form submissions added to your client spreadsheet
- **Form → confirmation email:** an automatic "thanks, I'll reply within one business day"
- **Calendar booking → prep task:** a task created for each discovery call
- **Invoice paid → thank-you draft:** created for you to review, not sent automatically

Keep a **human checkpoint** before anything reaches a client. Automations that send client emails without review are where embarrassing mistakes happen.

## You probably don't need to pay yet

Several automation tools have free tiers that cover a solo business's first few automations. When we checked their pricing pages (24 September 2026):

- **Make's** free plan offered up to 1,000 credits a month, 2 active scenarios, and a 15-minute minimum interval between runs.
- **Zapier's** free plan offered 100 tasks a month with two-step Zaps.

Limits change, so check before building. For a fuller comparison, including self-hosted n8n, see [Make vs Zapier vs n8n for freelancers](/blog/make-vs-zapier-vs-n8n/).

## Measure, don't assume

We can't tell you how much time you'll save, and nobody honestly can: it depends on your clients, tools and habits. So measure: re-estimate the minutes for your top five tasks after two weeks with the new workflow, and compare. Keep what helped and drop what didn't.

## Do it this week

1. List last month's recurring tasks (20 minutes).
2. Score them and sort by priority.
3. Pick the **top one** and decide: eliminate, template, AI draft, automate or keep human.
4. Fix only that one this week. Next week, the next one.

**The takeaway:** score before you automate. The best first fix is usually a template or an AI draft for a frequent, low-risk task, not a complex automation.
