Make, Zapier and n8n all connect apps so information moves without copy-paste: a form submission becomes a spreadsheet row, a booking becomes a task, a paid invoice triggers a thank-you draft. For a one-person business, the real question isn't "which is best?" but **which fits how you work, and whether you need one yet.**

**How we researched this:** we compared the three tools' official pricing pages on 24 September 2026 and describe their general approach. This is a **researched** comparison, not a hands-on test, and we'll say so wherever it matters. We have **no affiliate relationship** with any of them at the time of writing.

## First: do you need automation yet?

Automation pays off for tasks that are frequent, rule-based, low-risk and already written down as a process. If you haven't scored your admin tasks, start with [what to automate first](/blog/what-to-automate-first/). Often a template or an AI draft is the better first fix.

## The short version

| | **Zapier** | **Make** | **n8n** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Best for | Simple, quick connections between popular apps | Visual, multi-step workflows on a budget | Technical users who want control or self-hosting |
| Free option (checked 24 Sept 2026) | 100 tasks/month; two-step Zaps; 15-min polling | Up to 1,000 credits/month; 2 active scenarios; 15-min minimum interval | Free self-hosted Community Edition; cloud plans have a free trial |
| Cheapest paid (as listed) | Professional from $19.99/month | Core $12/month (10k credits, monthly billing) | Starter $20/month billed annually (2.5K executions) |
| Learning curve | Lowest | Moderate | Highest (especially self-hosted) |

*Prices as displayed to a US visitor on the vendors' pricing pages; currencies and plan details can vary by location and change over time. Check each page before deciding.*

## Zapier: simplest to start

**What it is:** the best-known automation tool, built around "Zaps": when something happens in app A, do something in app B.

**What the free plan offered (checked 24 Sept 2026):** 100 tasks a month, two-step Zaps (one trigger, one action) and 15-minute polling. The Professional plan, listed from $19.99 a month, adds multi-step Zaps.

**Good fit if:** you want one or two simple automations, such as "new form response → add a spreadsheet row", and you value simplicity over price. Two-step Zaps cover many solo-business needs.

**Watch out for:** the free plan's two-step limit means anything more complex (e.g., add a row *and* send yourself a notification) needs a paid plan.

## Make: visual and budget-friendly

**What it is:** a visual workflow builder where you connect modules on a canvas, with branching, filters and data transformation.

**What the free plan offered (checked 24 Sept 2026):** up to 1,000 credits a month, 2 active scenarios, and a 15-minute minimum interval between runs. The Core plan was listed at $12 a month for 10,000 credits (monthly billing), with a discount for annual billing.

**Good fit if:** you want multi-step workflows without paying yet, and you're comfortable with a visual builder that shows how data flows.

**Watch out for:** the visual canvas has more concepts to learn than Zapier. With 2 active scenarios on the free plan, choose your automations carefully.

## n8n: control and self-hosting

**What it is:** a workflow automation tool you can use as a hosted cloud service or **self-host** on your own server.

**What the pricing page offered (checked 24 Sept 2026):** a free, self-hosted Community Edition, and cloud plans starting with Starter at $20 a month billed annually (2.5K executions a month), with a free trial that didn't require a credit card.

**Good fit if:** you're technical (or enjoy learning), want to keep data on infrastructure you control, or expect to run many workflows where per-task pricing would add up.

**Watch out for:** self-hosting means you're responsible for the server, updates, security and backups. For most non-technical freelancers, that's more admin, not less.

## Which should you choose?

- **"I want one simple automation, today."** → Zapier free, if two steps are enough.
- **"I want a few multi-step workflows and don't want to pay yet."** → Make free.
- **"I'm technical and want control, or I'll run lots of workflows."** → n8n (self-hosted or cloud).
- **"I'm not sure what to automate."** → none yet. [Score your tasks](/blog/what-to-automate-first/) and write the process as an [SOP](/blog/sop-template-one-person-business/) first.

## Good first automations (any tool)

1. **Intake form → tracker row** (plus a notification to you)
2. **Booking → preparation task** in your task list
3. **Paid invoice → thank-you email draft** that you review before sending

Keep a **human checkpoint** before anything reaches a client. Automations that email clients without review are where mistakes become visible.

## A note on AI inside automations

All three tools can call AI models as part of a workflow (for example, drafting a reply from a form submission). If you do this, apply the same [client data privacy](/blog/client-data-privacy-ai/) rules as in chat: send only what's needed, and keep a human review step.

**The takeaway:** Zapier for the simplest start, Make for multi-step workflows on a budget, n8n for technical control. And for many solo businesses, the right automation tool this month is none, until the process is worth it.
