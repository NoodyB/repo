The moment a client says "yes" is when most freelancers relax, and when most client relationships quietly go wrong. Missing logins, an unsigned agreement, an unpaid deposit, a kickoff call nobody prepared for. None of it is dramatic. All of it costs time.

A good onboarding process fixes that with a **repeatable checklist**: the same steps, in the same order, for every client. This guide gives you that checklist, the free tools to run it, and an AI prompt that drafts the welcome email in your voice. Once the templates exist, a new client takes about 30 minutes of your time.

## What onboarding is for

Onboarding has three jobs:

1. **Make the agreement real.** Scope, price, timeline and payment terms in writing, signed or explicitly accepted.
2. **Collect what you need to start.** Access, files, contacts, brand assets, answers.
3. **Set expectations.** How you communicate, when you're available, how feedback works, and what happens next.

If those three are done before kickoff, most of the "surprises" that derail projects don't happen.

## The checklist

Copy this into your task manager, spreadsheet or notes app and run it for every new client.

### Before kickoff: paperwork and payment

- [ ] **Proposal accepted** in writing (a reply "Yes, let's go ahead with Option B" is better than nothing; a signed agreement is better still)
- [ ] **Agreement sent and signed**, covering scope, exclusions, timeline, payment terms, revisions, and what happens if the project pauses
- [ ] **Deposit invoice sent**, and work starts when it's paid (see [deposits and payment terms](/blog/deposits-and-payment-terms/))
- [ ] **Client added to your tracker** with the next action and date

### Welcome and collection

- [ ] **Welcome email sent** within one business day of the "yes" (prompt below)
- [ ] **Intake form sent**: goals, audience, examples they like and dislike, deadlines, decision-maker, and anything you must know (see [intake form tools](/blog/client-intake-form-tools/))
- [ ] **Access requested**: logins shared through a password manager's sharing feature rather than email, plus files and brand assets
- [ ] **Shared folder created** with a simple structure (01_Brief, 02_Working, 03_Delivered, 04_Admin)

### Expectations

- [ ] **Communication norms confirmed**: where messages go (email for decisions, chat for quick questions), your working hours and response time
- [ ] **Feedback process explained**: how many rounds, in what format, by when
- [ ] **Weekly update day set**: the day the client hears from you whether or not there's news (template in [the weekly client update](/blog/weekly-client-update-template/))
- [ ] **Kickoff call booked**, with an agenda sent 24 hours before

### Kickoff

- [ ] Walk through goals, scope and **what's not included**
- [ ] Confirm milestones and who approves each one
- [ ] Agree the first deliverable and date
- [ ] Send a written summary of the call the same day

## Run it in 30 minutes with free tools

You don't need client-portal software to do this well. A simple, free setup:

| Job | Free option | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Intake form | Tally or Google Forms | Tally's free plan includes unlimited forms and submissions (per its pricing page, checked 24 Sept 2026) |
| Agreement and proposal | A document template (Google Docs or Word) | Export to PDF and ask for written acceptance, or use your e-signature tool if you have one |
| Invoices | Your existing invoicing or accounting tool | Deposit invoice first |
| Folder | Google Drive, Dropbox or OneDrive | Same folder template every time |
| Tracker | A spreadsheet | One row per client with next action and date |

The 30 minutes break down roughly like this: 5 minutes to duplicate the folder and agreement template, 10 to personalize and send the agreement and deposit invoice, 10 to personalize and send the welcome email with the intake form, and 5 to update your tracker and book the kickoff. The templates carry the rest.

## Draft the welcome email with AI

Your welcome email should be warm, short and specific: what happens next, what you need from them, and by when. Here's a prompt that drafts it in your voice. It works in ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Copilot.

```text
Using my business brief and voice profile below, write a welcome email for a new client
(redacted details: """[PROJECT, TIMELINE, LINKS]"""). Include: a warm opening that references
the project; a numbered "what happens next" list (agreement, deposit, questionnaire link,
kickoff date); what I need from them and by when; how and when to reach me; and a friendly
close. Under 220 words. Use only the links and dates I provide. Write [NEED INFO] for anything
that is missing.

Business brief: """[BUSINESS BRIEF]"""
Voice profile: """[VOICE PROFILE]"""
```

Two things make this work. First, the **business brief and voice profile**: a one-page description of your business and a short description of how you write (see [the AI business brief](/blog/ai-business-brief/) and [making AI sound like you](/blog/make-ai-sound-like-you/)). Second, the **guardrail**: "use only the links and dates I provide" stops the assistant from inventing a kickoff date or a portal that doesn't exist.

Before pasting anything, replace the client's name and confidential details with placeholders. [Here's how to keep client data private with AI tools](/blog/client-data-privacy-ai/).

## Why the deposit belongs in onboarding

Onboarding is the natural moment to agree payment terms, because they're part of the agreement rather than an awkward conversation later. Payment problems are common: in Bonsai's data from over 100,000 freelancers, **29% of invoices were paid at least a day late**, and invoices over $20,000 were three times more likely to be late than invoices under $100 (Bonsai, updated January 2026). A deposit and clear milestones reduce the amount you have at risk at any time.

## Common mistakes

- **Starting work before the deposit clears.** It signals that your terms are optional.
- **Asking for everything in one giant email.** Put questions in a form, and keep the email to next steps.
- **Accepting passwords by email.** Ask clients to share access through a password manager or the platform's own user-invite feature.
- **No written summary after kickoff.** The summary is what you point back to when scope questions come up later.

## Make it a system

Save the checklist as a template, save your welcome email as a template, and only use AI to personalize the first lines for each client. After three or four clients, you'll notice which questions you keep asking by email. Add them to your intake form.
