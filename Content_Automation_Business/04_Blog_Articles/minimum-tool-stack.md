Tool overload is its own kind of admin. Every new app means another login, another subscription, another place information might be hiding. One solopreneur on Reddit put it as having "so many tools, subscriptions, workflows and 'systems'" that many people "seem more confused than ever".

This guide lists the **minimum** a one-person service business needs, the job each tool does, and free options, with free-plan limits checked on the providers' pricing pages. Start here, and add a tool only when a real problem demands it.

*No affiliate links: at the time of writing we have no affiliate relationship with any tool mentioned. If that changes, links will be clearly marked.*

## The principle: one tool per job

For each job below, pick **one** tool and make it the single place that job happens. Two invoicing tools, or clients tracked in three places, cause more problems than having no tool at all.

## The eight jobs

### 1. Email and calendar

**Job:** client communication and booking.
**Minimum:** a professional email address on your own domain (once you have one) and one calendar.
**Nice to have:** a booking link, so clients can pick a time without the back-and-forth.

### 2. Intake forms

**Job:** collect inquiry details and onboarding answers in a consistent format.
**Free options:** Tally, Google Forms, Jotform. Tally's pricing page listed a free plan with unlimited forms and unlimited submissions (checked 24 Sept 2026). Jotform's free Starter plan listed 5 forms and 100 monthly submissions. Our [comparison of intake form tools](/blog/client-intake-form-tools/) goes deeper.

### 3. Documents and files

**Job:** proposals, agreements, deliverables, templates.
**Minimum:** one cloud drive (Google Drive, OneDrive or Dropbox) with a **standard folder structure** for every client, e.g. 01_Brief, 02_Working, 03_Delivered, 04_Admin.

### 4. Client and project tracking

**Job:** know every client's stage, next action and date, and every project's milestones.
**Free options:** a spreadsheet (Google Sheets, Excel or LibreOffice) or Notion. Notion's pricing page listed a free plan for individuals with unlimited pages and blocks, 5 MB file uploads and 7-day page history. For how to choose, see [spreadsheet vs Notion vs CRM](/blog/spreadsheet-vs-notion-vs-crm/).

### 5. Invoicing and payments

**Job:** send invoices, take payment, record what's paid.
**Minimum:** one invoicing or accounting tool that fits your country's tax rules and your accountant's preferences. This is the one category where "whatever your accountant recommends" is usually the right answer. Track reminders in your tracker if your tool doesn't (see [our tested spreadsheet formulas](/blog/track-invoices-in-a-spreadsheet/)).

### 6. An AI assistant

**Job:** first drafts of emails, proposals, summaries and SOPs.
**Free options:** the free tiers of ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Copilot all handle client-email drafting. What matters more than the tool is your setup: a [business brief](/blog/ai-business-brief/), a [voice profile](/blog/make-ai-sound-like-you/), and a [privacy routine](/blog/client-data-privacy-ai/).

### 7. Email list (optional, once you market)

**Job:** stay in touch with past clients and prospects.
**Free options (checked 24 Sept 2026):** MailerLite's free plan listed 250 subscribers, 2,500 emails a month and 3 automations. Kit's free plan listed up to 10,000 subscribers with unlimited broadcasts, but its visual automation builder and automated sequences were paid features. See [MailerLite vs Kit vs beehiiv](/blog/mailerlite-vs-kit-vs-beehiiv/).

### 8. Automation (optional, later)

**Job:** move information between tools without copy-paste.
**Free options (checked 24 Sept 2026):** Make's free plan listed up to 1,000 credits a month and 2 active scenarios. Zapier's free plan listed 100 tasks a month with two-step Zaps. Add automation only after a process is [worth automating](/blog/what-to-automate-first/) and written down as an [SOP](/blog/sop-template-one-person-business/). Comparison: [Make vs Zapier vs n8n](/blog/make-vs-zapier-vs-n8n/).

## A complete $0 starter stack

| Job | Example free setup |
|---|---|
| Email & calendar | Your existing email and calendar |
| Intake | Tally or Google Forms |
| Files | One cloud drive with a folder template |
| Tracking | A spreadsheet |
| Invoicing | Your accountant's recommended tool (many have free tiers; check) |
| AI | A free AI assistant tier |
| Email list | MailerLite or Kit free plan, when you're ready |
| Automation | None yet, or one Make/Zapier free automation |

## When to add (or pay for) a tool

Add or upgrade only when you can name the problem:

- "I'm missing follow-ups because my tracker doesn't remind me" → maybe a CRM, or a better weekly review habit first
- "I copy form answers into my tracker by hand ten times a month" → maybe one automation
- "My list is past the free limit" → a paid email plan

And **remove** a tool when two tools do the same job. Your [weekly admin review](/blog/weekly-admin-review/) is a good time to ask: is every tool here earning its place?

## Watch the hidden costs

- **Subscription creep.** Many small monthly fees add up. List them once a quarter.
- **Data scattered across tools.** One tracker, one drive, one invoicing tool.
- **Free-plan limits.** They change. Re-check pricing pages before relying on a limit.

**The takeaway:** eight jobs, one tool each, mostly free to start. The system matters far more than the software.
