Chasing payment is the admin task freelancers put off most, and the one where putting it off costs the most. Late payment is common: in Bonsai's data from over 100,000 freelancers, **29% of invoices were paid at least a day late**, and invoices over $20,000 were three times more likely to be late than invoices under $100 (Bonsai, updated January 2026).

The fix is a **schedule decided in advance**, so every reminder is routine rather than personal. Here's a five-stage schedule, a template for each stage, and how to track it without stress.

## The five-stage schedule

| Stage | When | Tone | Goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | 3 days before the due date | Friendly heads-up | Make sure the invoice reached the right person |
| **B** | On the due date | Neutral reminder | Prompt payment today |
| **C** | 7 days overdue | Gentle nudge | Find out if something's wrong |
| **D** | 14 days overdue | Firm follow-up | Get a payment date in writing |
| **E** | 30 days overdue | Final notice | State next steps under your agreement |

Adjust the timing to your terms (for 30-day terms, some people move stage E later), but decide it **once** and apply it to every client. Consistency is what makes it feel professional rather than personal.

## Stage A: the heads-up (3 days before due)

Most late payments aren't deliberate. The invoice went to the wrong inbox, is waiting for a purchase order, or needs someone else's approval. Stage A catches those problems while there's still time.

```text
Subject: Invoice [#] – due [date]

Hi [Name],

A quick heads-up that invoice [#] for [project/milestone] ([amount]) is due on
[date]. I've attached it again for convenience. Payment details are on the invoice.

If it needs to go to someone else or needs a PO number, just let me know.

Thanks,
[Your name]
```

## Stage B: due date

```text
Subject: Invoice [#] due today

Hi [Name],

Just a reminder that invoice [#] ([amount]) is due today. You can pay by
[payment method/link]. Thanks!

[Your name]
```

Short. No apology for sending it.

## Stage C: 7 days overdue

```text
Subject: Invoice [#] – 7 days overdue

Hi [Name],

I haven't seen payment for invoice [#] ([amount], due [date]) yet. Could you check
it's in your payment run, or let me know if there's anything holding it up?

Invoice attached again. Thanks,
[Your name]
```

The question "anything holding it up?" invites the real answer: a missing PO, a query about the work, a cash-flow problem. You can't solve a problem you don't know about.

## Stage D: 14 days overdue

```text
Subject: Invoice [#] – 14 days overdue

Hi [Name],

Invoice [#] for [amount] is now 14 days overdue. Please could you confirm the date
you'll be paying it?

If there's a problem with the invoice or the work, I'd like to know so we can sort
it out. Otherwise, I'll expect payment by [date 5 business days from now].

[Your name]
```

The key move at stage D is **asking for a specific date in writing**. It changes the conversation from "someday" to a commitment.

## Stage E: final notice (30 days overdue)

```text
Subject: Final notice – invoice [#]

Hi [Name],

Invoice [#] for [amount] is now 30 days overdue, and I haven't received a reply to
my previous reminders.

As set out in our agreement, [the next step you agreed, e.g. "work on the remaining
milestones is paused until the balance is paid" / "late payment charges apply from
[date]"]. Please pay the balance by [date], or contact me today to agree a payment plan.

[Your name]
```

Only state consequences that are **in your agreement** and that you're prepared to follow through on. Rules on late-payment interest and fees vary by country and by whether your client is a business or a consumer. In the UK, for example, businesses can claim statutory interest on late commercial payments of 8% plus the Bank of England base rate (GOV.UK). Check what applies where you work before quoting any fee or interest, and get professional advice if a significant amount is at stake.

## Track it in one place

A schedule only works if you know which reminder is due for which invoice. You need one row per invoice with:

- Issue date, terms and **due date**
- **Paid date** (blank until paid)
- **Days overdue**
- **Reminder stage due** (A–E) and the **last reminder sent**

A spreadsheet handles this well. Our [step-by-step spreadsheet guide](/blog/track-invoices-in-a-spreadsheet/) includes tested formulas that calculate the stage automatically, and a free starter sheet. Check it during your [weekly admin review](/blog/weekly-admin-review/) and send whatever is due.

## Personalize with AI (optional)

Templates cover most cases. For a long-standing client or a sensitive situation, AI can adjust the tone:

```text
I need to send a payment reminder. Context (redacted): client relationship [NEW /
LONG-TERM / DIFFICULT], invoice [NUMBER] for [AMOUNT], due [DATE], currently [X] days
overdue, previous reminders sent: [LIST]. My agreement says: """[RELEVANT TERMS]""".
Draft the next reminder at stage [A/B/C/D/E]. Keep the tone [FRIENDLY / FIRM / FORMAL]
and under 120 words. Only mention fees or consequences that appear in my agreement text.
```

The last sentence is the guardrail that matters: AI shouldn't invent penalties or legal-sounding threats.

## Prevent the next late payment

Reminders treat the symptom. These reduce the cause:

- **Deposits and milestone billing** for larger projects (see [deposits and payment terms](/blog/deposits-and-payment-terms/))
- **Clear invoices** with exact due dates rather than "Net 14" (see [what to include on an invoice](/blog/what-to-include-on-an-invoice/))
- **Easy payment**: a link or clear bank details on every invoice
- **The right recipient**: ask during onboarding who pays invoices and whether they need a PO number

**The takeaway:** decide your reminder schedule once, template every stage, and track it in one place. Chasing payment then becomes a two-minute task, not a dreaded one.
