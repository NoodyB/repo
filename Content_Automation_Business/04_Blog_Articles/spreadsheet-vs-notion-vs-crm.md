Every solo business needs one place that answers three questions: **Who am I working with? What happens next with each of them? What am I owed?** Where that place lives matters less than having exactly one.

The three common options are a **spreadsheet**, **Notion** (or a similar all-in-one workspace), and a **dedicated CRM** or client-management tool. Each suits a different kind of business owner. Here's an honest look at the trade-offs.

*We have no affiliate relationship with any tool mentioned at the time of writing. We do sell a spreadsheet-based client tracker, so we've tried to be especially fair about the alternatives.*

## What you need to track (minimum)

Whatever you choose, it should hold:

- **Clients and leads:** name, stage (inquiry, proposal, active, past), value
- **Next action and date** for every client. This single field prevents most dropped balls
- **Projects:** milestones and dates
- **Invoices:** due date, paid date, and which reminder is due

## Option 1: A spreadsheet

**Strengths**
- Free (Google Sheets, LibreOffice) or already paid for (Excel)
- Flexible, fast and easy to export
- Formulas can calculate status, days overdue and next actions (see our [tested invoice-tracking formulas](/blog/track-invoices-in-a-spreadsheet/))
- Nothing to learn if you already use spreadsheets

**Weaknesses**
- No automatic reminders unless you build them or check it during a [weekly review](/blog/weekly-admin-review/)
- Easy to break formulas by typing in the wrong cell (color-code input vs. calculated columns)
- Doesn't send emails or invoices itself

**Best for:** people comfortable with spreadsheets, with up to a few dozen active clients and leads, who'd rather have a simple system they understand than features they won't use.

## Option 2: Notion (or a similar workspace)

**Strengths**
- Databases with multiple views (table, board, calendar)
- Client records can hold notes, files and linked projects on one page
- A free plan for individuals: Notion's pricing page listed unlimited pages and blocks for individuals, 5 MB file uploads and 7-day page history (checked 24 Sept 2026)

**Weaknesses**
- Easy to over-build. Complex templates are a common complaint: one r/Notion thread asks, "Who else finds Notion templates to be often overwhelming and complex?"
- Formulas and relations have a learning curve
- Like a spreadsheet, it won't chase clients for you without extra setup

**Best for:** people who already use Notion for notes and planning, and want client records alongside everything else, **provided they keep it simple.**

## Option 3: A dedicated CRM or client-management tool

This category includes general CRMs and freelancer-focused client-management tools that combine proposals, contracts, invoicing and scheduling.

**Strengths**
- Built-in reminders, pipelines and often invoicing and e-signature
- Client portals and automated workflows in some tools
- Less building. The structure is decided for you

**Weaknesses**
- Monthly subscriptions (check current pricing; it changes)
- More features than many solo businesses use
- Your data lives in their format, so check export options before committing

**Best for:** higher client volume, businesses that want proposals, contracts, invoices and scheduling in one paid tool, or anyone who has tried the simpler options and hit real limits.

## How to decide

Ask these questions in order:

1. **How many active clients and leads do you handle?** A handful to a few dozen: a spreadsheet or Notion is plenty. Many more: consider a CRM.
2. **Do you already use one of these daily?** Use the tool you already open every day.
3. **Will you actually check it weekly?** No tool works without a [weekly admin review](/blog/weekly-admin-review/). A CRM with reminders helps if you know you won't.
4. **Do you want invoicing and contracts in the same place?** If yes, a client-management tool may be worth paying for.

## Whatever you choose

- **One place only.** Don't split clients across tools.
- **Every client has a next action and a date.** Review them weekly.
- **Keep sensitive data minimal.** Don't store passwords or financial account details in your tracker.
- **Start simple.** You can always move to a more complex tool. Moving from a complex one back to simplicity is harder.

**The takeaway:** a spreadsheet for simplicity and control, Notion if it's already your home base (and you keep it simple), a CRM when volume or convenience justifies the subscription. The habit of checking it weekly matters more than the tool.
