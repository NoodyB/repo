### P-E01 · Day 1 · LinkedIn (text) · Why prompt packs disappoint

Why do big prompt packs disappoint?

Because a one-line prompt can't know:
→ what you're asking it to do (objective)
→ when it applies (use case)
→ what you'll paste in (inputs)
→ what "good" looks like (output format)
→ what it must never do (guardrails)

A useful prompt specifies all five. It's longer, but you use it again and again.

### P-E05 · Day 6 · LinkedIn (text) · Five decisions for every admin task

Every recurring admin task gets one of five decisions:

ELIMINATE: nobody would notice if you stopped
TEMPLATE: it's nearly identical every time
AI DRAFT: the structure repeats, the content changes
AUTOMATE: rule-based, frequent, low-risk and already templated
KEEP HUMAN: high risk, high judgment or relationship-critical

Notice automation comes after templating. If you can't write it as a checklist, you're not ready to automate it.

(From The Solo Admin Playbook, Chapter 2.)

### P-E13 · Day 24 · LinkedIn (text) · Testimonials, honestly

Three rules for testimonials:

1. Ask with specific, optional questions: situation before, working together, what changed.
2. Get permission to publish (name or anonymous, their choice) and let them approve the final wording.
3. Never write one for them, and never edit one in a way that changes its meaning.

In the U.S., the FTC's Endorsement Guides require endorsements to reflect the honest opinions of the endorser.
