## 2. Why these prices

**Evidence (snapshot, 2026-09-24; see the calculator's "Market price evidence" tab and `01_Market_Research/Marketplace_Observations_2026-09-24.csv`):**

- **Prompt packs** are cheap and crowded. Curated 100-prompt packs on Etsy were $5.49–$8.99, larger "templates" packs $14.67–$19, and bulk bundles as low as $0.20. So C is priced at the top of the curated range ($19). It is the smallest-ticket item and works best as a bundle add-on, not a stand-alone hero.
- **Client trackers** with Bestseller badges were $18.71–$24.99 (Excel CRM; Notion freelancer CRM $23.55). B does more than a tracker: a dashboard, reminder stages, an admin audit, a prompt log, an SOP index, a weekly review and a user guide. That supports $29, just above the observed range.
- **Guides and systems** span a wide range ($37 onboarding kit; $97 Notion business hub). A 38-page practical guide with worksheets at $24 sits in the accessible middle.
- **Bundle**: $49 is under the psychological $50 line and meaningfully cheaper than buying separately (32% off). It is the offer marketing should lead with.

**Positioning check.** The research assumption was that solo service providers will pay **$15–$50** for a system that saves admin time (Market Research §8, assumption 1: supported by adjacent pricing, **unproven** for these products). All prices sit inside that band.

**What to watch after launch (dashboard):** conversion rate by product page, bundle share of orders, refund rate and reasons. If bundle share is low, test a $45 bundle. If the refund rate on C is high, consider making C bundle-only.

## 3. Launch pricing done honestly

Showing a "was $49, now $39" price is only fair if $49 is a real price people have been offered. So:

1. **List every product at its regular price from day one.**
2. Give the launch price through a **Gumroad discount code** limited to a real window (Gumroad supports limiting a code's validity period and quantity, per its Help Center article on discount codes). Suggested code: `FOUNDING` (−$10 on the bundle, −$5 on A/B, −$4 on C), valid **14 days** from your launch date.
3. Say the true end date in emails and posts ("launch price until [DATE]"). When it ends, it ends. No fake countdowns, no extensions presented as "last chance".
4. Don't claim a product is "best-selling", "popular" or "loved by…" until real sales and reviews support it.

## 4. Refund policy (recommended)

> **14-day refund policy.** If the product isn't useful for your business, email within 14 days of purchase and you'll get a full refund. No forms, no hoops. Just tell us briefly what didn't work so we can improve it.

**Why:** trust matters more than a few refunds while there are no reviews yet. Gumroad also reserves the right to issue refunds within 90 days to prevent chargebacks, and it imposes a 30-day policy on accounts where more than 1% of customers dispute purchases (Gumroad Help, "Gumroad's refund policy"). A clear, generous policy keeps disputes low. Set it per product under **Settings → Specify a refund policy for this product** (Gumroad Help, "Set a custom refund policy").

## 5. Discounts, affiliates and upgrades

- **Upgrade path.** Customers who bought one product and want the bundle get a personal discount code worth what they paid (create it manually on request at first). Mention it in the post-purchase email.
- **Affiliates (later, optional).** Gumroad lets creators set affiliate commissions from 1% to 75%, with fees shared in proportion. A 30% commission on the $49 bundle leaves about $29.32 per affiliate sale (calculator: set the commission to 30%). Only recruit affiliates who have used the product. Require FTC-compliant disclosure from them.
- **No permanent sitewide sales.** Occasional, dated promotions (for example, a New Year "admin reset") are fine.
