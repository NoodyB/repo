# Workflow 5: Getting paid

!!! tip "In this chapter"
    Set clear payment terms, invoice consistently, and follow up on a predictable schedule with messages that stay friendly but firm. This is the workflow with the most direct effect on your cash flow.

## Prevent late payment before it starts

- **Agree terms before work starts.** Put the deposit, schedule and due dates in your agreement.
- **Take a deposit for new clients.** A deposit (commonly a percentage of the project) confirms commitment and funds the first phase.
- **Bill large projects in milestones.** In Bonsai's data, invoices over $20,000 were about three times more likely to be paid late than invoices under $100.^[2]^ Smaller, more frequent invoices reduce the damage when one is late.
- **Use exact due dates.** "Due 14 October" is clearer than "Net 14".
- **Make paying easy.** Include a payment link or clear bank details, the invoice number and a one-line description of what it covers.

!!! checklist "Every invoice includes"
    - [ ] Unique invoice number and issue date
    - [ ] Your business name and contact details; the client's billing name
    - [ ] Clear description of work (and milestone, if relevant)
    - [ ] Amount, currency and any tax lines required where you operate
    - [ ] Exact due date
    - [ ] How to pay (link or details)
    - [ ] Late-payment terms **only if they're in your agreement**

!!! warning "Late fees and interest"
    Whether you can charge late fees or interest, and how much, depends on your contract and your location. Some countries give businesses a statutory right to interest on late business-to-business payments (the UK is one example). Check your local rules and put your terms in writing before you rely on them.

## A predictable reminder cadence

Payment providers' guides recommend similar structures: a friendly reminder before the due date, a note on the due date, then progressively firmer messages at roughly one, two and four weeks overdue.^[8,9,10]^ Here's a version that fits a solo service business:

| Stage | When | Tone | Goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Heads-up | 3 days before due | Friendly | Prevent "I forgot" |
| B. Due today | Due date | Friendly | Make paying effortless |
| C. Gentle nudge | 7 days overdue | Polite, direct | Surface any problem |
| D. Firm follow-up | 14 days overdue | Firm, helpful | Offer a plan; set a date |
| E. Final notice | 30 days overdue | Formal | State next steps per your agreement |

*The Client Desk* workbook calculates **days overdue** and the **next reminder stage** for each invoice automatically, so you only need to look at one column during your weekly review.

## Reminder templates

!!! example "A. Heads-up (3 days before)"
    **Subject:** Invoice [NUMBER] due [DATE]

    Hi [NAME], a quick heads-up that invoice [NUMBER] for [AMOUNT] is due on [DATE]. You can pay here: [LINK]. Thanks!

!!! example "B. Due today"
    **Subject:** Invoice [NUMBER] is due today

    Hi [NAME], just a note that invoice [NUMBER] ([AMOUNT]) is due today. Here's the link again: [LINK]. If you've already paid, thank you, and please ignore this.

!!! example "C. Gentle nudge (7 days overdue)"
    **Subject:** Invoice [NUMBER]: now overdue

    Hi [NAME], I haven't seen payment for invoice [NUMBER] ([AMOUNT], due [DATE]) yet. Could you let me know when it's scheduled? If anything on the invoice needs correcting, tell me and I'll fix it right away. [LINK]

!!! example "D. Firm follow-up (14 days overdue)"
    **Subject:** Invoice [NUMBER] is 14 days overdue

    Hi [NAME], invoice [NUMBER] for [AMOUNT] is now 14 days past its due date of [DATE]. Please arrange payment by [NEW DATE]. If there's a cash-flow issue, reply and we can agree a short payment plan. [LINK]

!!! example "E. Final notice (30 days overdue)"
    **Subject:** Final notice: invoice [NUMBER]

    Hi [NAME], this is a final reminder that invoice [NUMBER] for [AMOUNT], due [DATE], remains unpaid despite earlier reminders. Please pay by [FINAL DATE]. As set out in our agreement, [NEXT STEP FROM YOUR AGREEMENT, e.g., "work is paused until the balance is cleared"]. I'd much rather resolve this directly, so please get in touch today.

!!! prompt "PAY-02 · Payment reminder sequence personalizer"
    I need to send a payment reminder. Context (redacted): client relationship [NEW / LONG-TERM / DIFFICULT], invoice [NUMBER] for [AMOUNT], due [DATE], currently [X] days overdue, previous reminders sent: [LIST]. My agreement says: """[RELEVANT TERMS]""". Draft the next reminder at stage [A/B/C/D/E]. Keep the tone [FRIENDLY / FIRM / FORMAL] and under 120 words, in my voice [VOICE PROFILE]. Only mention fees or consequences that appear in my agreement text. Include the payment link placeholder [LINK].

!!! checklist "Workflow 5 checklist"
    - [ ] Payment terms written into my agreement template
    - [ ] Deposit policy decided
    - [ ] Five reminder templates saved (A–E)
    - [ ] Invoice tracker with due dates and "days overdue"
    - [ ] Reminders reviewed every week during the admin review (Chapter 13)

# Workflow 6: Offboarding, testimonials and referrals

!!! tip "In this chapter"
    Finish projects in a way that makes clients want to come back and refer you: a clean handover, a specific testimonial request, and a scheduled check-in.

!!! checklist "Offboarding checklist"
    - [ ] Final deliverables sent, with a short handover document
    - [ ] Access transferred or removed (logins, shared folders, tools)
    - [ ] Final invoice sent; payment confirmed
    - [ ] Thank-you email with what to do if they need help later
    - [ ] Testimonial requested (with permission to publish)
    - [ ] Referral ask (optional, only if the project went well)
    - [ ] Files archived; tracker stage = Past client
    - [ ] 90-day check-in date added to the tracker

## Ask for a testimonial that's actually useful

Generic requests get generic replies ("Great to work with!"). Specific questions help clients write something honest and useful, and they make it easier for them to say yes.

!!! prompt "RET-02 · Testimonial request with specific questions"
    Draft a short email asking a client for a testimonial after we finished [PROJECT]. Make it easy: offer 3 optional questions (What was the situation before? What was it like working together? What changed afterwards?); say it can be 2–3 sentences; ask permission to publish it with their name and business (or anonymously, their choice); and say I'll send them the final wording to approve before it appears anywhere. Under 140 words, in my voice [VOICE PROFILE].

!!! warning "Keep testimonials honest"
    Publish testimonials only with permission, don't edit them in a way that changes their meaning, and never write or invent one. In the U.S., the FTC's Endorsement Guides require endorsements to reflect the honest opinions of the endorser.^[11]^

## The 90-day check-in

A short, no-pressure email three months after a project ends ("How's the new site working for you? Anything you need?") is one of the simplest ways to earn repeat work and referrals. Add the date to your tracker when you close the project.

# Build your SOP library

!!! tip "In this chapter"
    Standard operating procedures (SOPs) aren't just for big companies. In a business of one, an SOP is a note to your future self, and later to any assistant or contractor you hire.

## What a good solo SOP looks like

!!! example "SOP template"
    **SOP name:** [e.g., Onboard a new client]
    **Purpose:** [why this exists, in one sentence]
    **Trigger:** [what starts it, e.g., "Client accepts proposal"]
    **Time:** [typical minutes]
    **Tools & templates:** [links]
    **Steps:** [numbered, each starting with a verb]
    **Done when:** [the visible finish line]
    **Common mistakes:** [what to watch for]
    **Last reviewed:** [date]

## The "do it once, narrate it" method

1. Next time you do the task, keep a notes document open and jot each step as you go (or record a voice memo).
2. Paste the rough notes into your assistant with the prompt below.
3. Edit the result, save it, and link it from your tracker or task app.

!!! prompt "OPS-01 · SOP from rough notes"
    Turn my rough notes into an SOP using this template: SOP name, Purpose, Trigger, Time, Tools & templates, Steps (numbered, each starting with a verb, max 12), Done when, Common mistakes, Last reviewed [TODAY'S DATE]. Notes: """[NOTES]""". Keep my exact tool names and links. Where a step is unclear or seems to be missing, add [CHECK: question] instead of guessing.

# Light automation without new subscriptions

!!! tip "In this chapter"
    When (and when not) to automate, what you can do with tools you already have, and three simple "recipes".

## Four questions before you automate anything

1. **Is the task already templated and working manually?** If not, fix that first.
2. **Does it happen at least weekly?** Rare tasks rarely repay the setup time.
3. **Is it rule-based?** "When X happens, do Y" works; "use judgment" doesn't.
4. **What happens if it fails silently?** If the answer is "a client is upset" or "I lose money", add a check or keep it manual.

## Use what you already have

| Need | Tools you probably already have |
|---|---|
| Reusable email replies | Gmail templates, Outlook "My Templates"/Quick Parts, Apple Mail stationery, text expanders |
| Booking calls without back-and-forth | Booking features in your calendar app, or a free scheduling tool tier |
| Collecting intake info | Google Forms (responses can go straight into a Google Sheet), Microsoft Forms |
| Reminders | Calendar events or task-app due dates linked to your tracker's "next action date" |
| Invoice reminders | Many invoicing tools can send automatic reminders; check yours before building anything |

## Three starter recipes

!!! example "Recipe 1: Inquiry form → sheet → confirmation"
    **Trigger:** someone submits your website's inquiry form.
    **Steps:** response lands in a spreadsheet (native with Google Forms) → you get a notification → an auto-reply confirms receipt and shares your booking link.
    **Cost:** $0 with native form tools; a free automation tier if you need the auto-reply.
    **Human step:** you still send the personalized reply (Workflow 1) within one business day.

!!! example "Recipe 2: Proposal follow-ups that never slip"
    **Trigger:** you mark a proposal as sent in your tracker.
    **Steps:** set "next action date" to +3 days; your weekly review filters by this column; send the follow-up template.
    **Cost:** $0. *The Client Desk* does the date math for you.

!!! example "Recipe 3: The Friday update habit"
    **Trigger:** a recurring calendar event every Friday at 3 pm.
    **Steps:** open notes → run the weekly update prompt (DEL-01) per client → edit → send.
    **Cost:** $0. This isn't automation, but it removes the decision of *when*.

## What not to automate

- Anything that sends messages to clients **without you reviewing them**, such as AI-written emails going out automatically.
- Anything that makes financial commitments or applies fees automatically, unless your agreement is explicit.
- Conversations that need empathy: bad news, complaints, pricing negotiations.

# The weekly admin review

!!! tip "In this chapter"
    One 45-minute appointment with yourself each week keeps the whole system running and stops admin from leaking into every day.

## The agenda

| Block | Minutes | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Clear the decks | 10 | Process inbox and messages; capture tasks into your tracker |
| 2. Pipeline | 10 | Check every client's stage and next-action date; send due follow-ups |
| 3. Money | 10 | Invoices to send; reminders due (stages A–E); payments to record |
| 4. Next week | 10 | Block time for client work and deadlines; prepare update notes |
| 5. Improve one thing | 5 | Turn one annoying task into a template, SOP or prompt |

!!! prompt "OPS-05 · Weekly admin review assistant"
    Act as my weekly admin review assistant. I'll paste: my client tracker rows (redacted), my open invoices with due dates, and my calendar for next week. Produce: (1) follow-ups due this week, with the template to use; (2) invoice actions by reminder stage; (3) risks (overdue items, client dependencies, deadline clashes); (4) a suggested time-block plan for next week; (5) one improvement to my admin system based on patterns you notice. Ask me questions if something is unclear. Data: """[PASTE]"""

## Track four numbers

Keep it light. Once a month, note:

1. **Hours on admin this week** (a rough estimate is fine)
2. **Overdue invoices** (count and total)
3. **Average time to first reply** on new inquiries
4. **Proposals sent vs. accepted**

Watching these over two or three months tells you whether the system is working far better than any promise could.

## A final word

The goal isn't a business that runs itself. It's a business where admin is **predictable**: you know what needs doing, you have a good first draft ready, and you spend your best hours on the work only you can do. Start with the highest-scoring task from your audit, get one workflow running well, and build from there.
